> The world is overloaded with information. What it lacks is not information. It lacks firsthand material whose value has been tested. A virtual world this overbuilt turns each of us into an island, sunk inside our own bubble.
>
> Too little happens offline, so we fall into survivorship bias. Home and work, the same two points, conversations with people who share our background. It is easy to let one leaf hide the mountain, and to stay trapped inside our own technical view and our own reading of the business.
>
> Even when you meet someone online, it is usually a few sentences. You rarely get deep into a new technology or a new idea. Crazy Saturday is a chance for people from different backgrounds to sit together, trade what they know, and say what they think.
>
> You can come from frontend or backend, from algorithms, product, marketing, or operations. You can come from art, music, industry, education. To leave more time for people to meet and talk, Crazy Saturday has no stage and no host. The heart of it is wandering up to strangers, lightning talks, and a round table.
>
> What you share can take any form. A project. A technology or a current that interests you. A trend you have noticed. Even an idea that still lives only in your head. Anything you want to put in front of people, and talk through with them, belongs here.

## Wandering up to people

The first floor of the Design Capital Building, Xicheng, Beijing. I got to Kunlun Nest around noon.

![](https://files.guoqi.dev/images/202403180633265.webp)

At first I was a little stiff. I did not know what to do with myself. The room caught fire quickly.

After saying hello to [Kaiyi](https://kaiyi.cool) and [Dashu](https://dashu.ai), I poured a coffee, sat down, opened the laptop, and someone came over to talk.

![](https://files.guoqi.dev/images/202403180633800.webp)

Before long [Smart](https://x.com/xchaigl) arrived, and then [Langshu](https://x.com/i5ting).

Langshu is genuinely funny in person, an engineer who is actually interesting. Quietly: Smart's phone type is enormous.

![](https://files.guoqi.dev/images/202403180634767.webp)

Around two in the afternoon we got [Xinbao Otto](https://x.com/xinbao965), one of the speakers, and a host of the podcast [*Web Worker*](https://webworker.tech).

## Lightning talks

Everyone was in the room. Kaiyi hosted, and the lightning talks began.

![](https://files.guoqi.dev/images/202403180635380.webp)

### [Kaiyi](https://kaiyi.cool)

Kaiyi's title was *LLM! LLM! LLM!* Large language models. He started from the problem of lowering the similarity score on a paper, then walked through what an LLM actually does, what is worth caring about in this era, and how a person might live inside it. I took a lot away.

![](https://files.guoqi.dev/images/202403180638914.webp)

### [Dashu](https://dashu.ai)

https://thesoulsland.dashu.ai

Dashu's title was *Playing with Another Soul in the World*. The work is a startling "metaverse" site.

> Dashu once spent 182 days checking in at 47 universities, 55 provinces, and 160 cities. He has lived a lot, and he has had plenty of bright hours in work and in life.

The site is full of strange ways to play. You can buy land, meet people, play music, even hold a meeting. There are easter eggs still waiting for the community. Dashu said he has forgotten some of them himself.

![](https://files.guoqi.dev/images/202403180639004.webp)

### [Acui](https://x.com/cuixr1314)

https://julebu.co

Acui's title was *How to Build Your Own Product from Zero to One*.

earthworm is an open-source, collaborative, friendly tool for learning English. From the idea, to the making, to a sudden crowd of users and a real scale, to feedback and a community: Acui walked the whole path from zero to one in public.

The talk was dense the entire way. It is useful if you are freelance, building alone, or trying to be a one-person company.

![](https://files.guoqi.dev/images/202403180641373.webp)

### [Xinbao](https://x.com/xinbao965)

https://www.yuque.com/xinbao37/learn/mob8zk816xmqhih8

Xinbao's title was *Why Does Everyone Say Astro Is Pretty Good?*

"Coach, I want to learn this." Reforge the light of HTML and CSS. The duty is ours.

![](https://files.guoqi.dev/images/202403180642382.webp)

### [Jingliu](https://space.bilibili.com/23372292)

Jingliu's title was *How a Working Person Heals*.

[Watch on Bilibili](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV13A4y1d7KV)

> The film took three months to shoot. In that time I felt the season change more sharply. Trees put out buds and thickened. Willow fluff drifted in the street. Light fell on the grass. Getting my feeling back gave me a happiness that came from the inside.
>
> The largest thing I took from making it may be a store of inner strength: enough to keep loving ordinary life, and to stand up to the unpleasant things outside.

Love can outlast a long stretch of years. To enjoy the day you are in, and to love the life you have, is already a happy thing.

> A small plug. Xiao Sun loves ordinary life too.

[Watch on Bilibili](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1V6421u7Ry)

### Yafang

Yafang's title was *AI Technology Trends and AIGC in 2024*.

AI is absurdly hot, and her talk stayed at a high pitch the whole way. Follow Synced (机器之心) if you want the latest on AI.

![](https://files.guoqi.dev/images/202403180645031.webp)

### Wang Yi

Wang Yi mostly talked through his own working life and what he has made of it. From photography to software. That is a very cool turn.

![](https://files.guoqi.dev/images/202403180646912.webp)

### Yongwang

Yongwang's title was *A Survival Guide for Programmers*. Real estate is not what humans actually need. Living a very long time is.

He is a funny speaker, and the thinking underneath is tight, backed by careful numbers. It is a useful reference for the health of people who write software.

![](https://files.guoqi.dev/images/202403180647973.webp)

### [Langshu](https://x.com/i5ting)

Langshu closed the talks. His title was *Technical Practice for Independent Developers*.

He came at it from many sides and gave independent developers a direction, and a technical path to walk. Dense, the whole way through.

## Interesting people do find each other

Since graduation I have joined a lot of groups. Technology, blogs, work, hobbies.

Around the time I graduated I also made one of my own, "Xiao Sun and His Friends." The name borrows from "Luo and His Friends."

But...

Only recently did I see it. Too much information only raises the cost of finding the information that is actually worth something.

More groups is not better. We should go do things that are worth more. Going to an offline Crazy Saturday, for example.

## The Web Worker meetup

That evening I had barbecue with Xinbao, Smart, and Kaiyi. The alcohol loosened a long conversation about the podcast.

Web Worker is holding an offline meetup at the end of this month. Interesting people do find each other. I'll be looking forward to it.

The urge to write a blog hit me hard as soon as I started learning to program. Looking back, it came down to a few things.

- Once you step into programming, the water is deep. Learn a little, and you want it written down.
- A good memory loses to a bad notebook. What I learned yesterday may be gone in a few days. A blog is a log. It leaves a trail through the tinkering.
- I would look something up and land on someone else's post. Inner OS: what is this? If it were me, I could write it better.

## A short history of the tinkering

At first I tried writing on CSDN. People read, people followed, and the sense of achievement went straight to my head.

## What writing a blog has given me

### Heat

There is a sharp contrast in the blog world.

The sites of people who already know their craft are often very plain, and the writing in them is dense. Most newcomers spend their time on features and styling. The posts themselves are padded with water.

I think that is normal. I started the same way.

Writing that is worth anything does not burst out in a moment. It takes a long accumulation. Polishing the parts of a site that are not the writing can push a blogger toward better writing. How a site looks and what it says both matter.

The tinkering is for the sake of the work. Remaking the site, fussing with features, made me care more about the technology.

### Company

When I first started, I only read the official docs and the modification tutorials. Later I joined a few chat groups, and the gears began to turn.

Hit a bug, and someone in the group will help you through it. Face a choice, and people will offer something you can actually use. Idle chat kills time, and in a good room it also teaches you something and cuts a hole in the information bubble. I mean the good rooms.

For a while, [Akilar's Candy House](https://akilar.top) was the group I showed up in every day. I made friends there, and I met people who were far ahead of me.

### Settling

In school I did not take notes. I disliked it. It felt like a waste of time, and paper notes are hard to revise, hard to polish, hard to lay out. That was mostly high school. In college I did try electronic notes, and I used them hard.

A blog is different. You can revise as much as you like, go over a piece again and again, and make the page look the way you want. The main relief is that you no longer have to be ashamed of your handwriting.

A piece that actually has something in it is not easy. It costs the writer a great deal of time and attention. That is also why what settles out at the end is worth more.

In dull work, I run into something that interests me and want it laid out properly. In an ordinary stretch of life, a thought shows up and I want it kept.

Sort the knowledge. Write down what you think. What settles is the most valuable thing a life keeps.

## What the blog should be about

What, exactly, should a blog contain? That is the question.

At the start I wanted to write everything. New things I had learned. Study notes. Good essays I had read elsewhere, reposted. Lines and copy I liked, copied out.

That creates one serious problem. Chaos.

How do you fix chaos? You subtract.

When I rebuilt the blog I went through the old posts and kept about ten.

After that, it felt much more elegant.

## Afterword

They settle quietly, change without announcing it, grow slowly, and give without making a show of it.
